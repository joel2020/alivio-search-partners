The companies that succeed with LATAM engineering hubs treat the first 90 days as an integration project, not a hiring sprint. The ones that fail hire fast, onboard casually, and discover in month five that they built a vendor relationship with extra steps.

This is the playbook we run with clients, week by week.

## The playbook

**Days 1–15: Decisions before candidates.** Choose the anchor city (one, not three — density compounds), settle the EOR versus entity question (EOR until you pass roughly 25 heads), define the pod structure and its first two quarters of roadmap ownership, and set compensation bands at the top quartile of the local market. Every day spent here saves three later.

**Days 15–45: The build.** Run the search like an executive search, not a pipeline: a mapped market, structured technical assessment in the candidate's stronger language, and the hiring manager in every final round. Hire the tech lead first and let them close the rest — senior LATAM engineers join managers, not logos.

**Days 45–60: Employment and infrastructure.** Contracts through the EOR with IP assignment verified under local law, equipment shipped before day one, access provisioning treated as a security project, and a written working agreement covering hours, holidays (theirs, not just yours), and response-time expectations.

**Days 60–90: Integration, deliberately.** The hub's first sprint ships production code inside an existing team before the pod takes independent ownership. One US engineer pairs with the hub full-time for the first month. The standup moves to a time both sides attend live — which, in LATAM, it can.

**The scorecard.** By day 90 you should see: full attendance at shared ceremonies, first independent feature shipped, zero compliance exceptions, and voluntary attrition of zero. If any of those is red, the fix is almost always in the first 15 days' decisions.

## The mistake to avoid

Do not open with juniors to "test the model." A junior-heavy first pod tests nothing except your patience, confirms every offshore stereotype, and poisons the internal case for a strategy that works. Open with seniors, prove the model, then build the pyramid underneath it.
